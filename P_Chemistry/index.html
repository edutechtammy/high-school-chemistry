Standards and Objectives

Terms

  • chemistry: the study of matter
  • matter: anything that has mass and takes up space
  • accuracy: how close a measurement is to a truevalue
  • precision: how consistent and fine a scale of measurment the device gives
  • meniscus: the curved surface of liquid when in a narrow glass tube

Accuracy vs Precision

  • contrast accuracy vs prcision as an analogy (darts on a dartboard) and in measurement

Measurement and Uncertainty

  • demonstrate the ability to read from a glass and plastic graduated cylinder
    • Identify a glass graduated cylinder vs a plastic one based on which one has a meniscus
    • record a measurement to the mark plus one best guess digit using both a plastic and glass graduated cylinder
    • record a reading from a glass graduated cylinder correctly using the bottom of the meniscus
    • Give the reason for the presence of a plastic disk near the top of a glass graduated cylinder
    • state that part of the procedure of reading from the graduated cylinder is to get at eye level with the surface of the liquid
  • demonstrate the ability to read from rulers
    • record a measurement to the mark plus one best guess digit
    • Note and adjust placing items starting edge on the 0 mark of rulers that measure from the end of the ruler and from rulers that zero from a mark that is not on the edge of the ruler
  • record measurements from other devices (gauges, thermometers, etc.)
  • record readinggs from successive devices that shift in scale including scales that count by twos

Standards and Objectives

Always include the units with any numberical answer.

Base Units

  • gram (mass)
  • meter (length)
  • liter (volume)

Metric prefixes (most used prefixes for chemistry are bold and red)

  • tera
  • giga
  • mega
  • kilo (1,000x)
  • hecto
  • deka
  • BASE UNIT GOES HERE
  • deci
  • centi (x/100)
  • milli (x/1000)
  • micro
  • nano
  • pico

Mass vs weight: Mass is the same no matter where it is in the universe because it is independent of gravity.

Metric Bnechmarks

  • 1 gram ~ 1 fly
  • 1 kilogram ~ 2 boxes of butter plus 2 sticks
  • 1 liter = individual sport drink bottle
  • 1 cm ~ width of pinky fingernail
  • 1 m ~ floor to doorknob plus a piece of chalk
  • 1 km ~ a little over 1/2 mile
  • 0 degree Celsius = temperature at which water freezes
  • 100 degrees Celsius - temperature at which water boils
  • body temperature ~ 37 degrees Celsius

English Benchmarks

  • 1 pound ~ 1 box of butter
  • 1 ounce ~ 2 tablespoons of water
  • 1 gallon = 4 quarts
  • 1 quart = 2 pints
  • 1 pint = 2 cups
  • 1 inch ~ width of a bottle top on a 2 liter bottle
  • 1 ft ~ length of a rule
  • 1 yard ~ floor to doorknob
  • 1 yard = 3 ft
  • 1 f00t - 12 inches
  • 32 degrees Fahrenheit = water freezes
  • 212 degrees Fahrenheit = water boils
  • 98.6 ~ normal body temperature

Taring a scale means you place the container you will use to measure the mass of an object on the scale and press the tare button or turn it on (it will read 0 for the mass) with the container on it. This eliminates the mass of the container so what the scale reads when you add the material is just for that material.

1cc = 1cm3 = 1 mL

the base of the metric system is Celsius, but the base of the SI is Kelvin (we will use Celcius and Kelvin in Chemistry)

Writing units

  • Metric units do not have a period because they are symbols, not abbreviations
  • Use a period to abbreviate when using English units (oz., lbs., mi.)
  • Use lowercase with the excception of writing liters and millileters to avoid confusion with the numer 1 (L, mL)

Derived units are from mathematical operations (e.g. Area = m2, Density = g/m3)

Mac users can easily create the degree symbol by holding down the option button and zero.
